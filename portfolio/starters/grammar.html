—Singular and plural nouns⌄

I can talk about one thing or more than one.

one book / three books / two boxes

One thing or more than one

Use a singular noun for one thing and a plural noun for two or more. Most plurals end in -s. Some spelling rules change the ending.

OneMore than one
bookbooks
boxboxes
busbuses
babybabies
boyboys
Remember

After consonant + y, change y to ies. After vowel + y, just add s. Never add an apostrophe to make a plural.

Try it!

Write the plural: pencil, dress, story, toy.

pencils, dresses, stories, toys

Name six pictures, including singular and plural objects.

—Have + object + to-infinitive⌄

I can understand simple phrases about something to do.

I have a picture to colour.

Something to do

Use have + a thing + to + base verb to say there is something available to use or do.

Have + objectTo + verbFull sentence
I have a pictureto colourI have a picture to colour.
I have a bookto readI have a book to read.
She has a ballto play withShe has a ball to play with.
We have a songto singWe have a song to sing.
Remember

A book to read describes the book and what we can do with it. This is different from I have to read, which means I must read.

Try it!

Complete: I have a picture ___ (draw). I have a book ___ (read).

to draw; to read

How to demonstrate mastery

Match and say four phrases about familiar things to do.

—Everyday expressions⌄

I can respond naturally in familiar situations.

Here you are. / Me too! / So do I. / What now?

English for everyday conversations

Learn these short phrases as complete expressions. Use them with a friend or your teacher.

SituationWhat to say
GreetingHello! / Good morning!
Giving somethingHere you are.
Receiving somethingThank you.
Agreeing with a positive statementMe too! / So do I.
BirthdayHappy birthday!
Asking what to do nextWhat now?
Asking politelyPlease.
Saying goodbyeBye! / See you!
Remember

So do I can reply to I like apples. Me too! is a useful short alternative.

—Exclamations and stories about activities⌄

I can understand simple excited descriptions and story topics.

What a funny cat! / A story about riding a bike.

React and talk about stories

Use What a/an + adjective + singular noun to show surprise or excitement. Use about + an -ing form to say what a story is about.

PatternExample
What a + adjective + noun!What a funny cat!
What an + adjective + noun!What an interesting book!
A story about + verb-ingA story about riding a bike.
A story about + verb-ingA story about playing football.
Remember

Choose a or an by the next sound. After about, use riding, not ride, in these examples.

Try it!

Complete: What ___ funny dog! A story about (swim).

a; swimming

How to demonstrate mastery

Make two exclamations and identify two story topics.
