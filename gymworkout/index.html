Make the plan work

Arm-priority edition: 10 direct weekly sets for biceps and 10 for triceps, in addition to pulling and pressing. This is a starting target, not a requirement to train through fatigue. The rest of the plan stays balanced.

Your week

Mon Upper A · Tue Lower A · Wed recovery · Thu Upper B · Fri Lower B · weekend recovery. This plan assumes no additional hard training.

Light walking or mobility is optional. If you miss a day, continue the sequence rather than doubling hard sessions.

Start at the right dose

If returning after a break, use roughly two working sets per exercise for weeks 1–2, around 3 RIR, even when the full programme calls for three. Build up to the listed sets when recovery is good; arm work is deliberately higher than before.

Warm up for 5–8 minutes, then use 2–4 progressively heavier, non-fatiguing warm-up sets for the first big lift. Add brief warm-up sets before other unfamiliar or heavy movements.

What RIR means

Reps in reserve = how many more controlled repetitions you could complete. At 2 RIR, you could do roughly two more. Estimates take practice.

Use 2–3 RIR initially, then mostly 1–2. Keep Romanian deadlifts at 2–3. You do not need to reach failure. A muscle burn does not tell you your RIR.

When to add weight

For 3 × 6–10, use the same weight until all three sets reach 10 with your target RIR and consistent technique. Then use the smallest available increase.

Example: 24 kg dumbbells → 10/9/8 → 10/10/9 → 10/10/10. Increase next time. If the new weight puts you below six controlled reps, the jump is too large.

When progress stalls

One poor workout is normal. If an exercise stalls for three exposures, first check technique, sleep, food and rest times. Do not immediately add more arm sets.

If performance falls across several lifts for 1–2 weeks and fatigue persists, take an easier week: roughly half the sets, with 3–4 RIR. Persistent or worsening pain needs assessment, not a forced progression.

Food that supports training

A practical protein target is about 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day: roughly 130–160 g if you are still around 80 kg. Spread it across 3–4 meals. Include carbohydrate, fats, fruit and vegetables.

Use a seven-day average morning weight. If deliberately gaining, a conservative starting aim is about 0.1–0.2 kg/week. Adjust a little food after 2–3 weeks of trends, not one weigh-in. Weight gain is not all muscle; a surplus is not essential for everyone.

Recovery and technique

No injuries are assumed. Use controlled, comfortable ranges of motion, including a deep but manageable stretch on curls and extensions. If pain develops, adjust the movement rather than pushing through it.

The two upper sessions are longer because arms are a priority. Alternating a biceps exercise with a triceps exercise can save time without sacrificing quality; take longer rests if performance drops.

Use the log consistently

For dumbbells, enter the weight of one dumbbell. For barbells, include the bar. Machine numbers are comparable only on the same machine. On unilateral lifts, record repetitions per side and note any difference.

Arm progression: stay at 10 direct sets per muscle per week for at least 6–8 weeks. Aim to add reps, then load. If elbows or shoulders become irritated or performance declines, reduce arm isolation volume to 6–8 sets for a week or two. More sets are not automatically better.

Why this structure?

The upper/lower split spreads leg work and manages session length; it is a practical choice, not a claim that one split is universally best. The starting volume can be adjusted: Schoenfeld et al., weekly volume meta-analysis. Protein guidance draws on Morton et al., protein and resistance training meta-analysis. Exact exercise choices, schedule and weight-gain target are coaching choices rather than directly tested prescriptions for you.
